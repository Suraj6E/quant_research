# fxpit — An Expert Review

**Review by Fable 5** — 2026-08-06

Reviewed from two vantage points as requested: a quantitative researcher who has seen
point-in-time infrastructure built (and faked) at funds, and an ML practitioner who
spends most days fighting the same enemy this project names — temporal leakage.

Basis for this review: `planning.md` (the spec), `docs/findings.md`,
`docs/preregistration.md`, `docs/architecture.md`, `docs/ui-design.md`, `tests/SPEC.md`,
the README, and direct reading of the source (`src/fxpit/query/as_of.py`,
`src/fxpit/experiment/*`, the web layer). The non-integration test suite was executed
during this review: **95 passed, 0 failed, 21 deselected (integration)** — the green
claim is verified first-hand, at least for the fixture-backed subset.

---

## 0. Verdict, up front

**Yes, this project has merit — real merit, of a specific and somewhat unusual kind.**
It is not a product, it is not a strategy, and its flagship experiment is only
one-third complete by its own accounting. What it *is* is one of the most disciplined
demonstrations of research-data hygiene I have seen at portfolio scale, with two or
three outputs that are genuinely useful to people other than its author.

The one-line summary of the whole review: **the apparatus is excellent, the epistemics
are exceptional, and the empirical payload is thin.** The project's honesty about that
thinness is itself its strongest feature — and also the thing that most needs to stop
being a feature and start being fixed, because "we honestly report that we didn't get
the data" is admirable exactly once.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Data-engineering discipline | Excellent — invariants enforced structurally, not by convention |
| Statistical honesty | Excellent — refuses to render verdicts it cannot support |
| Empirical completion | Weak — 8 of 72 events priced; 1 of 3 channels measured; success criterion #3 unmet |
| Novelty | Modest but real — the power-calculation reframing and the ALFRED silent-failure finding |
| Real-world applicability | Indirect but genuine (see §4) |
| Code risk | Every phase commit is labelled "generated by claude - not reviewd" — no human review pass has occurred |

## 1. What it is

A three-tier database (Postgres for reference/vintage data, ClickHouse for ticks,
DuckDB as the sole query layer) whose single organizing idea is that **every read goes
through one implementation of `known_at <= t`** (`src/fxpit/query/as_of.py`). On top of
it: a tick ingest pipeline with a crash-safe ledger, an additive quality-flag layer, a
bitemporal macro store loaded with 586k real RTDSM vintage observations, a
timezone-correct session calendar, an ECB drift anchor with demonstrated (and honestly
bounded) detection power, and a pre-registered four-arm contamination experiment that
decomposes backtest inflation into revision leakage, timestamp coarsening, and the
mid-price assumption.

The actual empirical results, stated plainly:

- **One measured number:** the mid-price assumption costs +0.357 bps per round trip on
  EURUSD at a 30-minute hold (t = 7.56, corroborated by the independently measured
  0.271 bps median spread). This is a real measurement and the pairing logic that makes
  it resolvable at n = 8 is correct — the paired difference is nearly deterministic.
- **One unplanned insight:** the revision-leakage channel delivers its effect through
  rare signal flips (SD of paired differences: 26 bps vs 0.13 bps for the spread
  channel), so resolving a 2 bps revision effect needs ~682 events — roughly 28 years
  of payrolls + CPI. This is the project's most transferable finding.
- **Everything else:** unresolved, and said so. H1 and H2 not assessed; H4 blocked.

It also produced several empirical source findings of independent value: the keyless
ALFRED endpoint silently ignoring `vintage_date` (byte-identical payloads across four
requested vintages), Dukascopy's ask-before-bid wire order, zero-based month indices,
HTTP-200-empty-body weekends, and the RTDSM Sitecore hash-rotation gotcha.

## 2. What it should have been (vs what it is)

Judged against its own `planning.md`, the honest gap list:

| Promised | Delivered | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| 7 majors from 2015 (Path C decision) | Ticks for the days around 8 events in 2022 (~830k ticks total across samples) | The archive is a sliver. "2–5 billion rows projected" is a projection with <0.1% realized |
| 72-event contamination experiment | 8 events priced before Dukascopy IP-blocked the ingest | The flagship deliverable is a pilot run |
| Two independent feeds, documented disagreement rate (success criterion #3) | ECB daily fix only; HistData unretrievable without a headless browser | One of five success criteria partially met; H4 dead in the water |
| Revision-test threshold of 50 series-periods against real data | `MIN_REVISED_PERIODS_FIXTURE = 8`, still not raised despite real vintages being loaded since Phase 3 | An acknowledged loose end that costs nothing to fix and hasn't been |
| Release-time precision via BLS/BEA schedule join | 100% of the RTDSM archive still at month precision; Phase 6 routes around it via ALFRED dates + documented 08:30 ET | The macro store itself cannot yet support intraday `as_of` queries; only the experiment's side-path can |
| ECB/BIS non-USD macro coverage | Not loaded | Every non-USD-referenced question is out of reach |

What it *should have been*, in the stronger sense of "what would the ideal version of
this exact idea look like": the same apparatus pointed at **240+ events (2015–2024),
several instruments spanning a spread range** (EURUSD's 0.27 bps median spread is the
least interesting instrument on which to measure the mid-price channel — the finding
"the spread channel is small" is a statement about EURUSD, not about the channel), and
a completed cross-feed reconciliation. None of that requires new ideas. It requires
patience with a rate limiter.

## 3. What it is pretending to be — and what it honestly is not

This project is unusually free of pretense. The pre-registration is hash-stamped, the
underpowered flag is a structural property of the result object rather than a footnote
(`Experiment.underpowered`, `src/fxpit/experiment/run.py:133`), the −1h drift-anchor
miss is reported rather than hidden, and success criterion #3 is recorded as partially
met rather than redefined. That said, a reviewer being paid to be unkind would flag
four things:

1. **The scale costume.** The architecture is dressed for 2–5 billion rows; the
   resident data is under a million ticks. Three databases, a Postgres→ClickHouse
   mirror-export with a documented staleness footgun, and a materialized-view replay
   dance are complexity purchased against a projection that the free-source constraint
   itself may prevent from ever materializing (a full 7-pair × 10-year ingest is
   ~600k hourly requests against a source that blocks after ~360 in a burst — feasible
   paced over weeks, but nobody has done it). `planning.md` is candid that ClickHouse
   was chosen partly as a "CV signal", which is honest, but honesty about a costume
   does not make it not a costume. A single DuckDB over Parquet would serve the
   realized scale with a third of the operational surface.

2. **"116+ tests, green" flatters the validation depth.** The suite is genuinely good
   — the five-way mutation check (drop the `known_at` filter, invert vintage ordering,
   etc.) is a practice most production shops don't do — but the majority of tests run
   against hand-built fixtures. The fixture-backed suite proves the *logic* is right;
   only the 21 integration tests touch real stores, and nothing at all tests the
   system at even 1% of projected volume.

3. **The prose outruns the payload.** `docs/findings.md` is beautifully written —
   "a null result that explains its own null is not a failed experiment" — and every
   individual sentence in it is defensible. But the cumulative effect of that polish
   is a document that reads like a completed study when it describes a pilot with
   n = 8. The narrative craftsmanship is doing load-bearing work that data should be
   doing. A skeptical reader notices.

4. **No human has reviewed the code.** The git log says so explicitly: every phase
   commit ends in "generated by claude - not reviewd". The project's own thesis is
   that plausible-looking wrong output is the default failure mode of unchecked
   systems. That thesis applies to code generators too. The mutation checks and
   acceptance suite mitigate this substantially — better than most human-written
   projects mitigate it — but "the tests are green" and "a qualified human has read
   the diff" are different claims, and only one of them is currently true.

What it is *not* pretending to be, to its credit: profitable, complete, novel science,
or a product. All four disclaimers appear in the README above the fold.

## 4. Does it have real-world use? Where?

**Not directly, as software.** No fund would deploy a free-data, single-node,
7-pair store, and none needs to — every serious shop already owns (or believes it
owns) point-in-time infrastructure.

**Indirectly, in five real places:**

1. **Backtest audit and model validation.** The four-regime decomposition (honest /
   revised / date-only / mid-price) is a ready-made *audit protocol*. Model-validation
   teams at banks (SR 11-7 world) and allocators doing due diligence on external
   managers ask exactly these questions informally; this project turns them into a
   runnable harness. The most useful artifact here is not the code — it is the
   checklist implied by the variant table.

2. **ML feature stores.** The `known_at <= t` join is byte-for-byte the
   "point-in-time correct training join" problem that Feast, Tecton, and every
   in-house feature store solves or fails to solve. The failure modes this project
   catalogues — date-granularity truncation, silent coercion of a date to midnight,
   a "quick" accessor that bypasses the filter — are the exact mechanisms of
   train/serve skew and target leakage in production ML. §6 below expands on this.

3. **Real-time macro research.** The ALFRED keyless-endpoint finding (accepts
   `vintage_date`, silently serves current revised data, byte-identical across four
   requested vintages) is a **genuinely useful public-interest result**. Any academic
   or practitioner who built a "real-time" study on the keyless CSV has contaminated
   data and no error message telling them so. This deserves to be written up
   standalone; it is the single most citable thing in the repository.

4. **Teaching.** As a pedagogical artifact for "how research data actually goes
   wrong" — the base-year rebasing trap, the UTC+5:45 silent timezone bug, the
   materialized-view-doesn't-backfill gotcha — this is better than any textbook
   treatment I know, because every trap in it was actually stepped in and the
   footprints are preserved.

5. **The power-calculation framing.** "How much data does anyone need before they can
   honestly claim to have measured revision leakage?" (answer: more than most
   published event studies have) is a legitimate methodological point in the
   Croushore–Stark real-time-data tradition. It is currently buried in §5 of a
   findings doc nobody will find.

## 5. Technical review — the quant's reading

### What is correct and better than industry-typical

- **One implementation of the filter.** `_MACRO_SQL` in `as_of.py` is the only place
  `known_at <= t` exists, and a documented rule forbids a second. Most real systems
  fail precisely here — three teams, three "as of" implementations, one of them wrong.
- **Conservative timestamp placement.** Coarse `known_at` placed at the *latest*
  consistent instant, so the system withholds rather than leaks. This is the right
  asymmetry and almost nobody gets it right; the common industry choice (midnight of
  the vintage date) leaks up to 24 hours.
- **NULL as a fact.** A release published as missing keeps its `known_at` and ranks
  normally. Correct and rare.
- **The paired-difference estimand.** Comparing four Sharpes at n = 8 would be
  numerology; pairing on shared events and reporting per-channel deltas with SEs is
  the statistically right design, and the findings doc explains *why* the mid-price
  channel resolves when the others cannot (variance of the difference, not n). That
  explanation is textbook-quality.
- **The drift anchor's honesty about its own power.** Demonstrating it detects +1h and
  +5h45m, reporting that it misses −1h, and deliberately *not* asserting the −1h case
  in tests is a level of calibration discipline I rarely see in professional code.

### Where the expert eyebrow goes up

- **The t-statistics assume the wrong distribution for the revision channel.** The
  channel's own mechanism — rare sign flips, huge conditional impact — means the
  paired differences are a mixture: mostly ≈0, occasionally ±2× the event move.
  A t-test on that mixture at any realistic n is fragile. The fix is also the more
  interesting design: **decompose revision leakage into P(flip) × E[impact | flip]**.
  Flip probability needs no tick data at all — it is estimable from the vintage
  archive alone, and RTDSM goes back to 1964, giving ~700 payrolls observations
  *today*. Conditional impact is an ordinary event-study quantity. This two-part
  estimator would shortcut most of the 28-year data requirement the findings doc
  treats as a wall. That the findings doc computes the brute-force power requirement
  but doesn't notice the factorization is the clearest single place where an expert
  collaborator would have improved the result.
- **The synthetic surprise limits external validity of the revision channel
  specifically.** The prereg's argument — "a shared flaw is a constant across arms and
  cannot manufacture a between-arm difference" — is airtight for the spread and
  timestamp channels, but *not* for revision leakage, because that channel's magnitude
  is precisely "how often does the surprise sign differ between first-print and
  revised series", which depends on the surprise construction. A consensus-based
  surprise (were one available) could flip more or less often than the
  deviation-from-trend measure. The measured revision channel is therefore
  conditional on the synthetic measure in a way the document does not fully
  acknowledge.
- **Sample selection in the 8 events.** The ingest was cut off mid-run, so the priced
  events are the chronologically earliest ones — a non-random subsample. Immaterial at
  n = 8 (nothing is interpretable anyway), but the completed run should confirm the
  event set wasn't availability-biased.
- **EURUSD-only conditionality.** "The mid-price channel is small" is true of the
  tightest-spread instrument in the world at a 30-minute hold. The same harness on
  USDTRY or on a 2-minute hold would tell a very different story, and the
  cross-instrument gradient *is* the finding a practitioner would actually want. The
  docs gesture at this; the experiment doesn't do it.
- **Sharpe annualized by √24 events** is a stated convention and internally consistent,
  but produces numbers (2.2 at n = 8) that look like trading claims to any casual
  reader despite the underpowered banner. Reporting mean bps with CIs and dropping
  Sharpe entirely below the interpretability threshold would be cleaner than printing
  a Sharpe and disclaiming it.

## 6. Technical review — the ML expert's reading

There is **no machine learning in this project**, and that is a defensible choice —
the scope guardrails explicitly forbid strategy sophistication. But since the question
was asked from an ML vantage: this project is best understood as **the data
infrastructure that makes financial ML honest**, and its relevance to ML is larger
than its author seems to claim.

- The `as_of(t)` contract is isomorphic to the point-in-time-correct training join.
  `test_a_date_only_query_is_not_accepted` is the same bug class as training a model
  on features timestamped by date while serving on features timestamped by
  millisecond — the most common silent leak in production financial ML.
- The four contamination arms map onto ML leakage taxonomy one-to-one: revision
  leakage = label/feature versioning leakage; timestamp coarsening = temporal
  granularity leakage; mid-price = evaluation-cost misspecification. The experiment is,
  in ML terms, an **ablation study of leakage sources on a fixed model** — a design ML
  papers on leakage mostly lack, because they rarely control the arms to differ in
  exactly one dimension. A test enforcing single-dimension variant differences is
  something I would happily see in an ML reproducibility paper.
- The obvious ML extension that would multiply the project's audience: train an
  identical, deliberately dull model (logistic regression on a handful of features)
  under each of the four regimes and report the *validation-metric inflation* per
  regime. That converts "backtest contamination" into "leaderboard contamination,"
  which is the same result addressed to a community ten times larger.

What is missing from an ML-rigor standpoint: no data versioning beyond the ledger
(a DVC-style content hash of the loaded archive would make "the run used this data"
checkable the way the prereg hash makes "the run used this rule" checkable), and no
CI-enforced check that `MIN_EVENTS_FOR_INTERPRETATION` gates every rendering surface
(the CLI banner and the dashboard refusal exist; a test pinning both does too, per the
docs — good — but the threshold of 20 is itself a judgement call stated as such).

## 7. What it needs to be *good* (finish what is promised)

Everything here is closing gaps against the project's own spec. No new ideas required.

1. **Run the ingest, paced.** 64 remaining release days at a Dukascopy-respecting
   cadence. This is the single highest-value action available; every unmeasured
   channel is blocked on it and the ledger already makes it a resume, not a rebuild.
2. **Raise `MIN_REVISED_PERIODS_FIXTURE` from 8 to 50.** Real vintages are loaded;
   the spec requires it; it is one line plus whatever the suite then reveals.
3. **Land the BLS `.xls` release-schedule join** so EMPLOY vintages upgrade from
   month precision to date (and, for the modern era, time). Right now the macro store
   proper cannot answer the intraday questions the project exists for — only the
   Phase 6 side-path can.
4. **A human review pass over the code.** The commits say it hasn't happened. Given
   the project's own thesis about plausible-looking wrong output, this is not
   optional hygiene; it is the project applying its standard to itself.
5. **Screenshots in the README.** The dashboard has, by its own admission, never been
   looked at (§10 below). Open it, look at it, fix what's ugly, and show it.

## 8. What it needs to be *great*

1. **Extend to 2015–2024 and to 3–4 instruments spanning a spread range.** ~240
   events per instrument, and the mid-price channel becomes a *curve* (cost vs spread
   regime) instead of a point. The cross-instrument gradient is the practitioner
   deliverable.
2. **The flip-decomposition estimator for revision leakage** (§5). P(sign flip) from
   six decades of vintage data, conditional impact from the event study. This turns
   the "28 years needed" wall into a solvable estimation problem and would be the
   project's first genuinely novel methodological contribution.
3. **Replace t-tests with block-bootstrap CIs** on the paired differences at full
   sample, given heavy tails and the mixture distribution of the revision channel.
4. **Solve the second feed.** Either accept the headless-browser dependency for
   HistData behind an optional extra (the constraint forbids accounts, not browsers),
   or find another account-free tick source. H4 — the cross-feed disagreement rate —
   is arguably the most interesting promised number still missing.
5. **Content-hash the data as the prereg is hashed.** A result stamped with
   (rule hash, data hash) is a reproducibility claim; with only the rule hash it is
   half of one.
6. **The ML arm** (§6): identical simple model trained per regime, validation-metric
   inflation reported per channel.

## 9. What it needs to be *attention-worthy* to other experts

Attention is earned by artifacts other people can use or must respond to. Three
candidates, in order of effort-to-impact:

1. **Publish the ALFRED silent-vintage failure as a standalone note** (blog post at
   minimum; a short SSRN note is defensible). Title it something like "Your
   real-time macro data may be current data: a silent failure in keyless ALFRED
   access." Include the byte-identical-payload evidence. This is the one finding
   where the project knows something the field demonstrably needs to know, given how
   many real-time studies exist.
2. **Extract the acceptance suite into a reusable point-in-time correctness kit** —
   a pytest package anyone can point at their own store by implementing a small
   adapter (the four families: no-clairvoyance, revision, tick sanity, cross-feed;
   plus the mutation checks). Nothing like this exists as open source. It would be
   used by feature-store teams and quant-data teams alike, and it is 80% written.
3. **Write up the completed experiment + the flip-decomposition method** as a proper
   working paper in the real-time-data-literature register: "How much data does it
   take to measure backtest contamination? Channel-dependent power in event-study
   backtests." The finding that contamination channels differ by *two orders of
   magnitude in measurability* — and that most published event-study samples cannot
   resolve the revision channel they implicitly claim to handle — is a reviewable
   claim with teeth.

What will *not* earn attention: the infrastructure itself. Three-tier stores and
bitemporal schemas are competent, not novel. The epistemics — prereg hashes,
structural underpowered flags, provenance-typed dashboards — are the differentiated
material, and they only become visible to others through the three artifacts above.

## 10. UI review

Reviewed from `docs/ui-design.md` and the `src/fxpit/web/` source (~1,800 lines of
Python, ~2,400 of templates/CSS, zero external dependencies).

### What is genuinely excellent

- **Provenance as a required type** is the best idea in the UI and I have not seen it
  elsewhere: `Panel` raises without a provenance; a DEMO panel raises unless it names
  the phase that replaces it; the badge is rendered by the layout macro so no template
  can omit it; demo cards are dashed *and* hatched so a screenshot pasted into a slide
  still announces itself. This is the project's data ethics extended into pixels, and
  it is coherent all the way down.
- **Chart + `TableView` from the same call**, with the table required by the type
  system. Accessibility that cannot drift from the picture, and it doubles as the
  documented relief for the light-mode contrast WARN. Most production dashboards at
  actual funds do not meet this bar.
- **The palette was validated by script, not by eye** — all-pairs CVD ΔE checked in
  both modes, the one WARN documented with a structural mitigation, and a hard
  `MAX_SERIES = 3` cap where the palette stops clearing the floor. The negative-bar
  incident (a crossed-quote spread rendered outside the viewBox, fixed with a proper
  zero baseline rather than clamped away — because that bar *is the point of the
  panel*) is the design document's best moment.
- **Prospectus-first information architecture** — leading with the research document
  rather than the status board — is the right call for a project whose interesting
  claim is differences between regimes, not any single number.
- **No external requests, anywhere** — no CDN, fonts, chart library, or telemetry —
  honors the project's defining constraint in a layer where nobody would have checked.

### What holds it back

- **Nobody has ever looked at it.** The design doc says so in bold: no browser was
  available; geometry checks substitute for the most likely mechanical failure, but
  "typography, rhythm, or whether the thing actually looks good" is unjudged. For a
  UI this is the difference between designed and specified. One Playwright pass
  producing screenshots (which then belong in the README) closes this, and a visual
  regression baseline keeps it closed.
- **The hand-rolled 40-line htmx shim** is a small liability wearing a big library's
  name. It is honestly documented, but a file called `htmx.min.js` that is not htmx
  will eventually surprise someone. Rename it (`hx-shim.js`) or vendor the real thing.
- **`demo.py` survives against its own rule.** All seven phases are built, the
  design doc calls deleting this module "a project milestone, not a chore," and the
  routes proudly note the generators were deleted — yet the module remains because
  Phase 6 still imports `demo.hypotheses()`. Hypothesis text is static content, not
  synthetic data; move it to a template or content module and take the milestone.
- **The 3-series cap will collide with the 7-major universe** the moment a coverage
  or flag-distribution panel wants all pairs at once. The cap is correct (the palette
  genuinely stops working); the missing piece is the sanctioned alternative — small
  multiples or a sequential-scale matrix — designed before the need arrives, since
  the design doc's own "one shared filter row" note shows the authors know deferred
  UI abstractions need a named owner.
- **Single-user, no auth, localhost** — appropriate to scope and worth one explicit
  sentence in the docs so nobody deploys it otherwise.

### UI verdict

As a dashboard, unverified visually and therefore unfinished. As a *design system for
honest reporting* — provenance typing, enforced accessible twins, validated color,
demo data that cannot masquerade — it is the strongest part of the project after the
query layer, and the pattern is worth extracting and reusing independently of fxpit.

## 11. Scorecard

| Area | Grade | One line |
|---|---|---|
| Bitemporal core / `as_of` | A | Single filter implementation, conservative placement, NULL-as-fact — correct in the places industry gets wrong |
| Ingest & ledger | A− | claim→insert→settle ordering and honest gotcha log; unproven at projected scale |
| Cleaning / flags | B+ | Additive, reversible, honest about blocked detectors; small realized sample |
| Macro store | B | Real vintages, but month-precision everywhere and the BLS join still owed |
| Sessions / calendar | A− | No UTC constants, exclusion constraints, a real caught bug with a regression test |
| Validation harness | B+ | Drift anchor with calibrated, honestly-bounded power; second feed missing |
| Experiment design | A− | Pre-registered, hash-stamped, single-dimension arms enforced by test |
| Experiment execution | C | n = 8 of 72; one channel measured; a pilot presented with finished-study polish |
| Statistics | B | Right estimand and honest power math; wrong distributional tool for the revision channel and a missed factorization |
| Docs & epistemics | A | The best thing here; occasionally more polished than the evidence underneath |
| UI | B+ | Exceptional design system, never visually verified |
| Real-world utility | B− | Indirect but genuine: audit protocol, test kit, the ALFRED finding, teaching |

**Bottom line.** This is a serious piece of work whose value is currently locked up in
its least visible parts. Finish the ingest, apply human review, extract the three
publishable artifacts (§9), and it graduates from "impressive portfolio project" to
"thing other experts cite." Leave it as is, and it remains what it honestly says it is:
a well-built instrument that has taken one good measurement.
