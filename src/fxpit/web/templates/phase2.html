Phase 2 — Cleaning as a reversible layer

Each flag is an independent, re-runnable detector writing rows to tick_flag. Raw is never modified. A detector that turns out to be wrong is corrected by deleting its flags and re-running — not by re-downloading 300 GB, and never by editing the ticks.

Exit criterion: for any instrument-day, produce a list of every tick that was flagged and why. ✓ met See the worked example below, or python -m fxpit.flags --explain EURUSD 2024-01-08.

Why this is reversible

{{ m.card_open('Flags are disposable; raw is not', 'recorded', '') }}

The two halves of the pipeline use opposite idempotency strategies, and the asymmetry is the point. Ingest never re-fetches a settled hour — raw data is expensive and immutable. Detectors always recompute their own scope, deleting their prior flags before inserting — flags are cheap and disposable.

That makes "delete the flags and re-run" the only correction path rather than an exceptional one. A detector whose threshold turns out to be wrong costs a re-scan, not a re-download.

Flags live in a separate table rather than as columns on tick_raw because the taxonomy grows as new pathologies are found, and one tick can carry several flags at once. Columns would force a migration per discovery and could not represent overlap.
